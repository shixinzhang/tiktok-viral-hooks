### Core Principle
#### If you can't explain a concept simply enough for a child, you don't truly understand it
### Edgar Dale's Learning Pyramid (1940s)
#### Passive learning (lectures, reading) = lowest retention
#### Active learning (discussion, practice, teaching others) = much higher retention
#### Teaching others = highest mastery level
### Practical Application
#### Explain new knowledge in the simplest words
#### Identify stumbling blocks as areas needing review

## Strategy 2: Value Review and Spaced Repetition
### Hermann Ebbinghaus's Memory Research (1885)
#### Self-tested with nonsense syllables
#### Findings: ~75% forgotten after 2 days if no review
#### Forgetting curve: rapid at first, then slows (steep cliff → gentle slope)
### Spaced Repetition Strategy
#### Review just before forgetting, not after
#### Each review slows the forgetting rate
#### Increasing intervals: 1 day → 3 days → 1 week → half a month
### Mechanism
#### Recalling memory signals importance to the brain
#### Strengthens long-term memory

## Strategy 3: Active Retrieval Instead of Repetition
### Testing Effect / Retrieval Practice Effect
#### Actively recalling > rereading for long-term memory
### Roediger & Karpicke Experiment (2006, Washington University)
#### Group A: reread material 4 times
#### Group B: read once + 3 recall tests
#### Result: Group B significantly outperformed Group A after 1 week
### Flawed Traditional Study Habits
#### Rereading, underlining, note-copying create illusion of diligence
#### Actual effectiveness is low
### Better Approach
#### Close the book, use blank paper or question set
#### Force yourself to reproduce what you learned
#### Or mentally recall all content

## Deliberate Practice (Anders Ericsson)
### Research at Florida State University
#### Studied top performers: chess players, violinists, surgeons
### Common Trait
#### Not mechanical repetition
#### Constantly adjusting, practicing slightly beyond current ability
### Three Core Elements
#### Focus
#### Feedback
#### Error correction
### Berlin Music Academy Violin Study
#### Best students: 10,000+ hours by age 20
#### Average students: ~4,000 hours
#### Gap = quality and total practice time, not just talent
### 10,000-Hour Rule (Malcolm Gladwell, *Outliers*)
#### High-quality deliberate practice = path from average to excellence

#### Actual brain processing time: maybe under 1 hour

#### Confront what you don't understand
